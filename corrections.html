Accuracy and accountability

Corrections policy

This policy explains how UK Toll Crossing Guide checks its information, responds to reported errors and records significant corrections.

Our approach

Commitment to accurate information

UK Toll Crossing Guide aims to provide clear, accurate and current information about UK toll roads, bridges, tunnels, payment arrangements, concessions and notice procedures.

Reasonable efforts are made to check important information against official operator websites, government publications, legislation, tribunal information and other authoritative sources.

Each completed crossing profile should display the date on which its information was last checked.

Information can change

Toll prices, payment deadlines, account systems, concessions and appeal procedures can change without notice. Visitors should always verify time-sensitive information with the relevant operator.

What can be reported?

Types of corrections

A correction report may concern:

  • An incorrect toll price
  • An incorrect payment deadline
  • A broken or outdated official link
  • An incorrect telephone number
  • An outdated account or payment method
  • Incorrect exemption or concession information
  • An inaccurate notice or appeal procedure
  • A spelling, grammar or formatting error
  • An accessibility problem
  • A page that does not work properly
  • Information attributed to the wrong operator
  • A statement that is unclear or potentially misleading

Reporting an error

What to include in a correction report

To help the issue be checked, provide:

  1. The title or web address of the affected page.
  2. The heading or section containing the possible error.
  3. The wording you believe is inaccurate or unclear.
  4. A short explanation of why you believe it should be changed.
  5. The corrected information you believe should be shown.
  6. A link to an official or authoritative supporting source, where available.
  7. The date on which you checked the supporting source.
Example correction report

Page: Humber Bridge
Section: Current charges
Possible error: The standard car toll is shown as £2.00.
Suggested correction: [state the corrected amount].
Source: [link to the operator’s current toll-price page].
Source checked: [date].

How to report

Submitting a correction through GitHub

Website errors can currently be reported through the public Issues section of the UK Toll Crossing Guide GitHub repository.

A GitHub account may be required to submit an issue. Reports and comments made there may be publicly visible and associated with the contributor’s GitHub username.

Open a new correction report on GitHub (opens in a new tab)
Do not post private information

GitHub Issues is a public area. Do not include your home address, telephone number, email address, vehicle registration, notice reference, account details, bank information or unredacted documents.

Do not upload toll notices, payment records, Blue Badges, benefit letters, vehicle documents or court papers.

Individual disputes

A correction report is not an appeal

The corrections process is only for reporting possible errors on UK Toll Crossing Guide.

It cannot:

  • Cancel a toll notice or Penalty Charge Notice
  • Submit an appeal to a toll operator
  • Place enforcement action on hold
  • Extend a payment or appeal deadline
  • Obtain a refund from an operator
  • Respond to a Letter of Claim
  • Respond to a county court claim
  • Provide individual legal representation
Official deadlines continue to apply

Do not wait for a website correction to be considered before paying, appealing or responding to a formal document. Follow the official instructions and deadline shown on your notice.

Evidence

How correction reports are assessed

A reported error will be considered against the strongest reasonably available evidence.

Sources may include:

  1. Legislation, confirmed byelaws and court decisions
  2. Official government, authority or tribunal information
  3. Official toll-operator websites and documents
  4. Written confirmation from the relevant operator
  5. Archived copies showing when published information changed
  6. Other reliable evidence relevant to the issue

A social-media post or personal report may alert the website to a possible issue, but it will not automatically be treated as proof of a general rule or widespread practice.

Source differences

When official information conflicts

There may be occasions where an operator’s website, terms, byelaws, notices or correspondence use different wording.

Where a difference cannot be resolved confidently, the website may:

  • Present the different wording separately
  • Identify the source of each statement
  • Explain why the difference may matter
  • Avoid presenting an uncertain interpretation as settled
  • Recommend following the earlier or safer deadline
  • Recommend obtaining individual advice where necessary
Interpretation is not confirmation

The website will distinguish between confirmed facts, official statements, legal wording, possible interpretations and reports made by members of the public.

Review process

What happens after an error is reported?

  1. The affected page and reported wording are identified.
  2. The supporting source is checked.
  3. Other authoritative sources may be compared.
  4. The information is corrected, clarified or left unchanged according to the available evidence.
  5. The page’s “last checked” date may be updated.
  6. A significant correction may be recorded publicly.

Submitting a correction does not guarantee that the suggested wording will be adopted. The final wording will be based on the available evidence and the website’s editorial approach.

Levels of change

Minor and significant corrections

Minor corrections

Minor spelling, punctuation, formatting or broken-link changes may be corrected without a separate correction notice.

These changes do not materially alter the meaning of the information.

Significant corrections

Changes involving toll prices, deadlines, exemption eligibility, appeal rights or enforcement procedures may be recorded with an explanatory note.

The note may explain what changed, when it changed and which source supported the correction.

Correction records

Publishing significant changes

Significant corrections may be recorded in the relevant GitHub issue, repository history or on the affected page.

A correction record may include:

  • The date of the correction
  • The affected page
  • A summary of the previous wording
  • The corrected information
  • The source relied upon

Personal information about the person who reported the issue will not be added to the website unless there is an appropriate reason and permission to do so.

Community reports

Reports about operator experiences

Reports from motorists may help identify recurring concerns involving payment systems, accounts, exemptions, notices or correspondence.

Individual reports will not automatically be presented as established facts about an operator’s general conduct.

Before publishing a wider claim, the website may seek:

  • More than one independently submitted report
  • Redacted supporting documentation
  • Official clarification from the operator
  • Published regulatory or court findings
  • Other independently verifiable evidence

Community information will be clearly distinguished from official rules and verified findings.

Fairness

Information about organisations and individuals

Corrections and community reports must be expressed fairly and supported by relevant evidence.

Reports should focus on the information or process in question and should not contain insults, threats or unsupported allegations against employees, organisations or other individuals.

Personal information about staff members, motorists or third parties should not be included in a public correction report.

Accessibility

Reporting a usability problem

Correction reports may also identify accessibility or usability problems, including:

  • Links that cannot be reached using a keyboard
  • Text that is difficult to read
  • Poor colour contrast
  • Missing headings or labels
  • Navigation problems on mobile devices
  • Information that is difficult to understand
  • Pages that do not work with assistive technology

When reporting an accessibility problem, identify the page, device, browser and assistive technology used where possible. Do not include unnecessary personal information.
