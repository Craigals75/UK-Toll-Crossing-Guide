Notice and appeal guidance

Received a toll notice?

Understand what document you have received, preserve your evidence and prepare a clear response using the correct appeal process.

Immediate action

What to do first

  1. Record the date on which you received the notice.
  2. Read every page and identify the deadline for payment, appeal or response.
  3. Check the notice reference, vehicle registration, crossing date, time and location.
  4. Identify the organisation that issued the document.
  5. Preserve payment records, account information, exemption documents, screenshots, emails and envelopes.
  6. Check the relevant crossing page on this website and then confirm the process on the operator’s official website.
Do not miss the deadline while making a complaint

A customer-service complaint may not count as a formal appeal or representation. Unless the operator confirms otherwise, follow the appeal instructions printed on the notice.

Identify the document

What type of notice have you received?

Toll notice or payment notice

This may be an Unpaid Toll Charge Notice, Unpaid Toll Notification or another operator-issued payment notice.

Follow the operator’s internal payment or appeal process and the deadline shown on the notice.

Penalty Charge Notice

Dart Charge and Merseyflow use a statutory Penalty Charge Notice process.

A formal challenge to the charging authority is normally called a representation. A rejected representation may provide a right of appeal to the Traffic Penalty Tribunal.

Debt-recovery letter

A debt-recovery company may be instructed to request payment after an earlier notice remains unresolved.

Check whether the letter is simply requesting payment or whether it is headed Letter of Claim or Letter Before Claim.

Letter of Claim

This indicates that court proceedings are being considered. It should contain a response deadline and information about how to reply.

Do not assume that an earlier appeal automatically stops this deadline.

County court claim

An official claim will contain a claim number and court response instructions.

You must respond using the method and deadline stated on the claim. An unanswered claim may result in judgment being entered against you.

Order for Recovery

This can occur during the statutory enforcement process used for certain Penalty Charge Notices.

It is not an ordinary operator appeal. Follow the instructions and deadline supplied with the order.

Important distinction

Not every toll notice uses the same appeal system

Statutory process

Dart Charge and Merseyflow

These crossings issue Penalty Charge Notices under road-user charging legislation.

The process normally begins with representations to the charging authority. If they are rejected, the recipient may be able to appeal to the independent Traffic Penalty Tribunal.

Operator process

Other toll operators

Operators including Warburton Toll Bridge, Tyne Tunnels, Humber Bridge and M6 Toll have their own notice and appeal arrangements.

Their notices should not automatically be treated as statutory Penalty Charge Notices, and the Traffic Penalty Tribunal may not have jurisdiction.

Use the terminology printed on your notice

Calling every notice a “fine” or “PCN” can cause confusion. Quote its exact title, reference number and issuing organisation in your response.

Supporting information

Evidence to collect

Submit evidence that directly supports what happened. Keep the original files and send copies unless the operator specifically requires otherwise.

  • Payment confirmation email
  • Operator receipt and payment reference
  • Bank or card statement showing the transaction
  • PayPoint or toll-booth receipt
  • Account transaction history
  • Account balance and automatic top-up records
  • Vehicle registration details held on the account
  • Screenshots of error messages or failed payment attempts
  • Browser history showing access to the payment website
  • Telephone records showing calls to the operator
  • Exemption or concession approval
  • Blue Badge or qualifying-benefit evidence where relevant
  • V5C or vehicle-transfer confirmation
  • Evidence showing where the vehicle was at the time
  • Copies of all notices and correspondence
  • The envelope in which the notice arrived
Bank statements

A bank transaction can show that money left your account, but it may not show which vehicle or crossing it covered. Where possible, include the operator’s receipt, confirmation number and vehicle registration alongside the statement.

You may redact unrelated transactions, but leave your name, relevant transaction, date, amount and payment recipient visible.

Preparing your response

How to write a clear appeal

Keep the appeal factual, chronological and focused on the reason the notice should be cancelled or reconsidered.

  1. Identify yourself, the notice and the vehicle.
  2. State clearly that you are challenging or appealing the notice.
  3. Give a short chronological account of what happened.
  4. Explain the exact reason you believe the notice is incorrect or should be cancelled.
  5. Refer to each supporting document by name.
  6. State what outcome you are requesting.
  7. Request written confirmation of the decision.
Only include truthful information

Remove any part of a template that does not apply. Do not guess, exaggerate or claim to have evidence that you do not possess.

Common circumstances

Points you may need to explain

The toll was paid

Give the payment date, time, amount, reference and vehicle registration entered. Attach the receipt and relevant bank transaction.

The payment system failed

Explain every attempt, including the approximate times, method used and error displayed. Include screenshots, browser history or telephone records where available.

The account had credit

Provide the account number, balance, vehicle details and transaction history. Explain whether automatic top-up was enabled.

An exemption applied

Provide the concession reference, approval date, registered vehicle and any renewal confirmation.

A Blue Badge alone does not necessarily provide an automatic exemption at every crossing.

Registration entered incorrectly

State the registration entered, the correct registration, payment details and why the error occurred. Ask whether the payment can be matched to the correct journey.

The vehicle was sold

Give the transfer date and provide DVLA or sale evidence. Do not identify another person as responsible unless the information is accurate.

The vehicle was not there

Explain why the vehicle could not have used the crossing. Consider cloned-registration evidence, photographs, location records or police reports where applicable.

The original notice was not received

State which document you first received and when. Confirm the address held by DVLA at the relevant time and provide supporting evidence if appropriate.

Non-receipt does not automatically cancel every notice, but it may affect the available procedure or the request you make.

Example wording

General toll-notice appeal template

Adapt this template to the operator’s official form. Text in square brackets must be replaced or removed.

Subject: Appeal against [exact notice title and reference]

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing to challenge the following notice:

Notice reference: [reference]
Vehicle registration: [registration]
Crossing date and time: [date and time]
Date notice received: [date]

I request that the notice is cancelled for the following reason:

[Give a short and accurate explanation of why you dispute the notice.]

The relevant sequence of events is:

1. [First relevant event and date]
2. [Second relevant event and date]
3. [When payment or registration was attempted]
4. [When the notice or later letter was received]

I have enclosed or uploaded the following evidence:

1. [Payment receipt or reference]
2. [Bank or account record]
3. [Screenshot, exemption approval or other evidence]

[If applicable: I did not receive the original notice. The first correspondence I received was [document] on [date]. My address as recorded by DVLA at the relevant time was [address or explanation].]

Please consider the facts and evidence supplied, cancel the notice and confirm the outcome in writing. If the appeal is rejected, please provide the reasons, the evidence relied upon and details of any further review or appeal process.

Yours faithfully,

[Full name]
[Postal address]
[Email address]
[Date]

Example wording

If you paid within the required period

I dispute this notice because I made payment for the crossing within the published payment period.

Payment of £[amount] was made on [date] at [time] using [website, application, PayPoint, telephone or account]. The payment reference is [reference], and the vehicle registration entered was [registration].

I have attached the payment confirmation and the relevant transaction record. Please trace the payment, explain how it was allocated and cancel the notice.

Example wording

If the payment system failed

I attempted to pay the toll on [date] at approximately [time], before the published deadline.

I used [website, application or telephone]. The system displayed [exact error message or description]. I then attempted payment again at [time] using [method].

I have attached [screenshots, browser history, telephone records or other evidence]. I ask that these documented payment attempts are considered and that the notice is cancelled.

A failed attempt does not guarantee cancellation. The operator will consider the facts, its rules and the evidence provided.

Example wording

If the original notice was not received

I did not receive the original notice referred to in your correspondence.

The first document I received was [document title], dated [date], which arrived on [date]. I therefore did not have the opportunity to respond to the original notice at the earlier stage.

[Explain whether the address held by DVLA was correct and provide any relevant evidence.]

Please provide a copy of the original notice, its date of issue, the address used and available evidence of dispatch. I also request that the matter is returned to the appropriate earlier stage so that my representations can be considered.

Later statutory documents require special care

If you have received an Order for Recovery, court claim or enforcement notice, do not rely only on this template. Follow the formal procedure and deadline stated on that document.

Sending the appeal

Before pressing submit

  • Use the official appeal portal or stated postal address
  • Check that the reference and registration are correct
  • Remove all unused template wording
  • Make sure every statement is accurate
  • Attach clearly named evidence files
  • Keep screenshots of every completed form page
  • Save the final appeal text before submitting
  • Keep the confirmation screen, email or postal receipt
  • Record the submission date and time
If sending by post

Retain a complete copy and obtain proof of posting. Check whether the notice requires the appeal to be received, rather than merely posted, by the deadline.

After the decision

If the appeal is rejected

  1. Read the reasons given for rejection.
  2. Check the new payment or further-appeal deadline.
  3. Check whether the operator considered each item of evidence.
  4. Identify whether additional evidence is available.
  5. Determine whether there is an independent appeal, further internal review or complaints process.
  6. Do not assume that submitting a complaint suspends the payment or enforcement deadline.
Dart Charge and Merseyflow

Following a Notice of Rejection of Representations, the recipient may be able to appeal to the Traffic Penalty Tribunal. Follow the appeal information and deadline included with the rejection notice.

Escalated cases

Debt recovery, court claims and enforcement

Never ignore formal documents

An unanswered Letter of Claim, county court claim, Order for Recovery or enforcement notice can progress even if you believe the original toll notice was incorrect.

Debt-recovery correspondence

Check the amount claimed, the original notice reference and the identity of the creditor. If the amount is disputed, explain why and provide copies of relevant evidence.

Letter of Claim

Follow the response instructions and deadline. Request any documents needed to understand the claim and consider obtaining independent debt or legal advice.

County court claim

Respond using the official court process stated on the claim. Do not send a response only to the toll operator or debt-recovery company.

Enforcement agent correspondence

An enforcement agent is not the same as an ordinary debt-collection company. Check the document, the authority relied upon and obtain urgent independent advice if necessary.

Protect your personal information

Do not publish an unredacted notice, bank statement, vehicle document or court form on social media.

Remove addresses, references, registration numbers, account details, barcodes and QR codes before sharing documents publicly.
